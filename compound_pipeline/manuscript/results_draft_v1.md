## 1. Spatial distribution of the three compound hazards

Over 1985–2024, 9,608,094 heatwaves (HW) were detected at the 54,615 PSU, about 4.4 per PSU and year, and 12,787,839 extreme precipitation (EPE) days were identified at the 53,782 PSU eligible for precipitation extremes. The three axes of compound hazard have distinct spatial patterns (Fig. 1).

Same-day co-occurrence of a heatwave and an EPE (Axis I) is rare overall: 0.076 days per PSU and year on average over the period, with the highest values in Central (0.120) and Eastern Africa (0.093), and much lower values in Western (0.058) and Southern Africa (0.021) (Fig. 1a; Supplementary Table S8). Hotspots include eastern Madagascar, the Great Lakes region and eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, the coasts of the Gulf of Guinea and Angola, whereas the Sahel shows very few co-occurrence days. Co-occurrence peaks near the equator (5°S–5°N) and declines towards the subtropics (Supplementary Fig. S4a).

The asymmetry of EPE around heatwaves (Axis II) shows the opposite geography (Fig. 1b). After/before count ratios are highest in Southern Africa and in eastern Africa south of the equator, and lowest in the Congo basin, where some PSU have ratios below 1. The latitudinal median of the ratio peaks near 22°S (about 2.7) and remains between 1.2 and 1.5 elsewhere (Supplementary Fig. S4b). Only 5 PSU had no EPE before their heatwaves (ratio undefined).

Heatwaves preceded by drought (Axis III) are most frequent in Southern Africa and in the western Sahel (Fig. 1c). Their share peaks at 22°S (about 37%) and again between 10° and 20°N, with a minimum near the equator (about 23%; Supplementary Fig. S4c). Across latitudes, Axis I therefore varies in opposition to Axes II and III (Supplementary Fig. S4d).

## 2. Temporal changes, 1985–2024

The number of heatwaves increased steeply over the period, from 62,110 in 1985 to 746,354 in 2024 at the eligible PSU (1.2 and 13.9 per PSU, respectively). Part of this contrast reflects the fixed 1985–2014 reference period (Methods).

Co-occurrence days increased at +0.030 days per PSU and year per decade (95% CI 0.014–0.056; Fig. 2a), with the largest trends in Central (+0.054 [0.017, 0.114]) and Eastern Africa (+0.037 [0.021, 0.067]) and a smaller one in Western Africa (+0.016 [0.006, 0.032]). In Southern Africa the trend was weak (+0.005 [−0.001, 0.014]). The increase accelerated after about 2010 and is concentrated in the last decade (Fig. 3a). The share of heatwaves containing at least one co-occurrence day increased much less (+0.23 percentage points per decade [0.05, 0.37]), indicating that the rise in co-occurrence mainly follows the increase in heatwave frequency.

In contrast, the EPE asymmetry around heatwaves showed no robust trend at the continental scale (−0.07 per decade [−0.18, 0.05]; Mann–Kendall p = 0.13) or in any region (Fig. 2b). Decadal ratios varied between 1.4 and 1.7 (Fig. 4c), and the spatial pattern of the asymmetry was stable across decades (Fig. 3b). The event-coincidence difference (trigger − precursor) decreased slightly (−0.010 per decade [−0.025, −0.001]; Fig. 2c).

The share of heatwaves preceded by drought increased by +2.83 percentage points per decade [1.23, 4.40] (Fig. 2d). The increase was strongest in Southern Africa (+8.88 [0.18, 18.71]), where the share reached 57.5% in 2015–2024 (Fig. 5c), and was also significant in Western (+3.84 [0.25, 7.46]) and Central Africa (+2.79 [0.39, 4.99]), but not in Eastern Africa (+1.16 [−2.40, 4.54]). The drought maps show the expansion of high shares over the Sahel and southern Africa after 2005 (Fig. 3c). This trend is not found with the precipitation-only SPI-3 (−0.86 per decade [−2.81, 0.65]) or with SPEI-3 in the onset month (+1.48 [−1.72, 3.23]) (Supplementary Fig. S6), indicating that it is driven by the evaporative demand term of SPEI rather than by precipitation (Section 5).

## 3. Heat and extreme rainfall co-occur less often than expected

Heatwave days and EPE days co-occur much less often than expected if they were independent. The likelihood multiplication factor was 0.23 when expectations were computed from annual frequencies and 0.32 (95% cluster-bootstrap CI 0.30–0.33) when they were conditioned on the day of the year. The seasonal LMF is higher than the naive one because heat and extreme rainfall peak in different seasons, but it remains well below 1: within the same time of year, heatwave days are about three times less likely than chance to coincide with an EPE. The cluster-bootstrap interval, which resamples ERA5 cells, is wider than the PSU-level interval (0.315–0.320), as expected from the sharing of temperature series between PSU. [Regional LMF: Supplementary Table S7.]

## 4. Extreme rainfall is more frequent after than before heatwaves

Among the 9,361,759 heatwaves with a complete ±30-day window, 2,368,613 EPE were attributed to the 30 days after a heatwave and 1,640,462 to the 30 days before, an after/before ratio of 1.44 (Fig. 4; Supplementary Fig. S1a). The ratio exceeded 1 in all regions: 1.22 in Western, 1.63 in Eastern, 1.21 in Central and 2.22 in Southern Africa. At the country level, the ratio ranged from 0.93 (Guinea, the only country below 1) to 2.61 (Namibia) (Fig. 4a; Supplementary Table S1).

**The asymmetry exceeds what the rainfall season produces.** In the seasonally constrained null model, where each heatwave keeps its location, duration and time of year but is moved to another year, the continental ratio was 1.014 (95% envelope 1.005–1.023). The observed ratio was therefore 1.42 times the null median, and lay far outside the null distribution (Supplementary Fig. S1d). The observed/null ratio was 1.21 in Western, 1.60 in Eastern, 1.20 in Central and 2.21 in Southern Africa (Supplementary Fig. S1a), and the observed ratio exceeded the null envelope in every season of heatwave onset (Supplementary Fig. S1c).

**The asymmetry comes mainly from a lack of rainfall before heatwaves.** Compared with the same season in other years, heatwaves were preceded by 37% fewer EPE (observed/null median 0.63), whereas EPE after heatwaves were only 10% fewer (0.90) (Supplementary Fig. S1e). This deficit before heatwaves was found in every region, and was strongest in Southern Africa (0.42), followed by Eastern (0.61), Western (0.67) and Central Africa (0.71). The number of EPE after heatwaves was close to the null in Eastern Africa (0.97) and below it elsewhere (0.81–0.92). Event coincidence analysis gave the same picture: the share of heatwaves with at least one EPE in the preceding 30 days was 0.195, well below the null (0.228–0.230), whereas the share with at least one EPE in the following 30 days (0.233) was close to the null (Supplementary Fig. S1b). The ratio greater than 1 therefore reflects drier-than-usual conditions before heatwaves, more than wetter-than-usual conditions after them.

**Timing and seasonality.** EPE frequency is low and nearly constant in the month before heatwave onset, peaks on the first day after the end of the heatwave and then decreases over the following weeks (Fig. 4b). The asymmetry follows the rainfall seasons (Supplementary Fig. S3b): in Western Africa the ratio reached 8.3 for heatwaves starting in February and 6.3 in May, before the monsoon, but fell to 0.1–0.5 for heatwaves starting from September to December, after the rains; in Southern Africa it reached 11 in September and 5.5 in October, before the rainy season, and 0.2–0.4 in April–May. The months with the highest asymmetry were also those with the highest share of heatwaves preceded by drought (Supplementary Fig. S3c).

**Robustness.** The ratio decreased with the length of the window, from 1.73 (±7 days) to 1.33 (±60 days), but remained above 1 in all regions and for all windows (Supplementary Fig. S5a,b). Because rainfall can end a heatwave, EPE on the day after the end could inflate the ratio: 7.9% of the post-heatwave EPE fell on that day. Excluding the EPE within 1, 2, 3 or 5 days of the heatwave boundaries reduced the ratio to 1.38, 1.38, 1.37 and 1.35, respectively [Supplementary Table S5]. In absolute terms, the numbers of EPE both before and after heatwaves increased over the period, in line with the number of heatwaves, while their ratio remained stable (Supplementary Fig. S2a).

## 5. Heatwaves are more often preceded by drought than locally expected

Of the heatwaves with an antecedent SPEI-3 value, 29.7% were preceded by drought (SPEI-3 < −1 in the month before onset) (Fig. 5). The share decreased with the severity of the drought threshold, from 45.4% (SPEI-3 < −0.5) to 16.3% (< −1.5) and 6.8% (< −2) (Supplementary Fig. S5c). It was highest in Southern Africa (42.5%) and similar in the other regions (27.5–29.4%).

Drought frequency itself varies across places and seasons, and the frequency of SPEI-3 < −1 over all PSU-months (19.5%) exceeds its nominal value of about 16%, because drought months become more frequent after 2014, the end of the calibration period, as evaporative demand increases (Supplementary Fig. S7). We therefore compared the observed share with the drought frequency expected locally (Supplementary Fig. S8; Supplementary Table S6). Given the drought frequency of the same PSU and calendar month, 19.8% of heatwaves would have been preceded by drought; given that of the same PSU, calendar month and decade, 23.5%. Heatwaves were therefore preceded by drought 1.26 times more often than expected from location, season and decade. With the precipitation-only SPI-3, the corresponding shares were 19.3% observed and 15.9% expected (×1.21). Thus, about half of the raw excess relative to the nominal 16% reflects where and when heatwaves occur and the background increase of SPEI drought, and the remainder is specific to heatwaves.

The heatwave-specific excess differs between regions. In Southern and Eastern Africa it was found with both indices and was at least as large with SPI-3 (×1.42 and ×1.33) as with SPEI-3 (×1.31 and ×1.36), indicating a genuine precipitation deficit before heatwaves. In Western and Central Africa, the SPEI-3 excess (×1.18 in both) was mostly not matched by SPI-3 (×1.07 and ×1.11), indicating that it mainly reflects evaporative demand. In Western Africa, where many heatwaves occur in the dry season, SPI-3 cannot reach values below −1 when rainfall totals are frequently zero, so this estimate is conservative.

Using the onset month instead of the preceding month raised the share of heatwaves in drought to about 38% (Supplementary Fig. S5e), consistent with the heatwave itself increasing evaporative demand in its own month, which supports the choice of the antecedent month.

Heatwaves preceded by drought lasted longer and were hotter than other heatwaves. The ratio of mean durations increased with drought severity, from 1.05 (SPEI-3 < −0.5) to 1.12 (< −2) (Supplementary Fig. S5d), and peak Tmax was higher, markedly in Western and Southern Africa and only slightly in Central Africa (Fig. 5a,b). ## 6. Compound-hazard regimes

Combining the three axes, 53,370 PSU (97.7%) could be classified for 1985–2024 (Fig. 6). The remaining 2.3% includes 1.5% of PSU not eligible for the analysis and 0.8% with insufficient support. Using the upper quartile of each axis as threshold, 44.0% of PSU had no prominent axis. Single-axis regimes were the most common otherwise: heat–precipitation co-occurrence only (15.2%), post-heatwave precipitation only (14.6%) and drought-heat only (13.9%). Among combinations, post-heatwave precipitation with drought-heat was the most frequent (9.0%), whereas co-occurrence combined with either of the other axes was rare (1.2–1.8%) and all three axes were prominent in only 0.2% of PSU.

These shares reflect the dependence between axes. Post-heatwave precipitation and drought-heat were prominent together 1.47 times more often than if the axes were independent, consistent with heatwaves occurring at the end of the dry season, just before the rains. In contrast, heat–precipitation co-occurrence was prominent together with post-heatwave precipitation 0.29 times, and with drought-heat 0.43 times, as often as expected: the places where heat and extreme rain occur on the same day are generally not those where heatwaves are preceded by drought and followed by rain. Spatially, the former are found in the humid tropics (Madagascar, the Gulf of Guinea, the Great Lakes), the latter in Southern Africa and Senegambia (Fig. 6b).

Applying the same thresholds to each decade, the share of PSU with heat–precipitation co-occurrence only increased from 3.6% in 1985–1994 to 30.0% in 2015–2024, and that of heat–precipitation with drought-heat from 1.3% to 9.9%, whereas post-heatwave precipitation only decreased from 26.0% to 7.7% and the share with no prominent axis from 39.9% to 19.4% (Fig. 6c). The same changes were found on the 18,564 PSU classifiable in both decades (heat–precipitation only from 3.6% to 33.6%). At the PSU level, however, the regime was persistent in only 18.3% of PSU, compared with 14.5% expected by chance, so these changes should be read as net shifts in the distribution of regimes (Fig. 6f). The shift towards co-occurrence regimes mainly follows the increase in heatwave frequency (Section 2), whereas part of the decrease of post-heatwave precipitation regimes may reflect the larger sampling variability of decadal ratios, which were based on few events in the first decade (median 8 attributed EPE per PSU in 1985–1994). With the upper tercile as threshold, the ranking of regimes and the direction of all decadal changes were unchanged (Supplementary Fig. S9).

## 7. Sensitivity to the heatwave threshold

With a single annual threshold instead of a calendar-day threshold, heatwaves concentrate in the hot season, and the results change accordingly (Supplementary Figs. S10–S13). Co-occurrence days were fewer (0.042 per PSU and year, mean of annual values) and the seasonal LMF lower (0.25 [0.24, 0.27]). The after/before ratio was much higher (3.11), but so was its null median (1.84 [1.81, 1.86]), because heatwaves in the hot season are followed by the onset of the rains in every year: the observed ratio was 1.69 times the null median. The deficit of EPE before heatwaves was nearly identical to that of the calendar mode (0.63 of the null), whereas EPE after heatwaves slightly exceeded the null (1.06). In this mode, 32% of PSU, mostly in the Sahel, had no EPE before their heatwaves, and the Western African ratio declined strongly over time (−2.78 per decade), as heatwaves defined by an annual threshold increasingly occurred outside the pre-monsoon season. The share of heatwaves preceded by drought (26.6%) was only slightly above its local expectation (×1.10 with SPEI-3, ×1.08 with SPI-3), and 35% of PSU could not be classified into regimes. The main conclusions — co-occurrence rarer than expected, an asymmetry driven by a deficit of rainfall before heatwaves, and a modest heatwave-specific drought precondition — hold in both modes.
